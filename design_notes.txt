1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To modernize inventory management, bulk quoting, and order fulfillment for Munder
Difflin Paper Company, I designed and implemented a four-agent hub-and-spoke
multi-agent system in Python using the `pydantic-ai` framework and SQLite
(`munder_difflin.db`).

Staying within the strict project constraint of at most five agents, the system
separates responsibilities across one Orchestrator Agent and three specialized
Worker Agents with zero overlapping duties:

  [1] Orchestrator Agent (`orchestrator_agent`)
      - Role: Customer-facing coordinator and execution router.
      - Responsibilities: Parses incoming text inquiries (extracting items,
        quantities, request date, and event delivery deadline), runs internal
        financial health checks, delegates domain tasks to specialist worker
        agents, enforces output safety/redaction rules (preventing exposure of
        internal cash reserves, profit margins, or stack traces), and compiles
        transparent customer responses.
      - Assigned Tool & Starter Helper:
          * `check_company_health` -> wraps `generate_financial_report()`

  [2] Inventory & Procurement Agent (`inventory_agent`)
      - Role: Catalog normalization, real-time stock tracking, and supplier
        restocking decisions.
      - Responsibilities: Maps informal customer item descriptions to canonical
        catalog items via `resolve_catalog_item()` and `ITEM_ALIASES`, checks
        available stock, estimates supplier restock arrival dates, verifies
        purchasing capacity, and places restock orders when inventory is low and
        supplier lead times meet the customer's deadline.
      - Assigned Tools & Starter Helpers:
          * `list_available_inventory` -> wraps `get_all_inventory()`
          * `check_item_stock` -> wraps `get_stock_level()`
          * `check_supplier_timeline` -> wraps `get_supplier_delivery_date()`
          * `reorder_stock` -> wraps `get_cash_balance()` and
            `create_transaction(..., transaction_type='stock_orders')`

  [3] Quoting & Pricing Agent (`quoting_agent`)
      - Role: Historical pricing analysis and competitive bulk-quote generation.
      - Responsibilities: Queries historical customer quotes for comparable
        event types and order sizes, looks up official unit prices from the
        `paper_supplies` catalog, applies tiered bulk discounts (5% for
        500-999 units, 10% for 1,000-4,999 units, 15% for 5,000+ units), and
        produces clear, itemized pricing explanations.
      - Assigned Tools & Starter Helpers:
          * `lookup_past_quotes` -> wraps `search_quote_history()`
          * `calculate_bulk_quote` -> uses `CATALOG_PRICES` (`paper_supplies`)

  [4] Sales & Fulfillment Agent (`sales_agent`)
      - Role: Final order validation and transaction recording.
      - Responsibilities: Performs a final verification of real-time stock
        levels and supplier delivery feasibility before recording confirmed
        customer sales in SQLite and updating cash and inventory balances.
      - Assigned Tools & Starter Helpers:
          * `finalize_customer_sale` -> wraps `get_stock_level()`,
            `get_supplier_delivery_date()`, and
            `create_transaction(..., transaction_type='sales')`

Architectural Decision-Making:
  - Why 4 Agents (Hub-and-Spoke): A 4-agent split cleanly mirrors standard
    enterprise operations (Front-Office Routing, Warehouse/Procurement, Pricing,
    and Order Execution) while staying below the 5-agent cap. Passing `ctx.usage`
    during sub-agent delegation (`consult_inventory_agent`, `consult_quoting_agent`,
    `consult_sales_agent`) ensures unified token accounting across the run.
  - Why `pydantic-ai`: Provides typed tool schemas, structured dependency/context
    injection, and explicit `UsageLimits(request_limit=None)` control so complex
    multi-item inquiries never fail prematurely on default request ceilings.


2. EVALUATION RESULTS & PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS (`test_results.csv`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The multi-agent system was evaluated end-to-end against all 20 sequential
customer inquiries in `quote_requests_sample.csv` (spanning April 1, 2025 to
April 17, 2025), starting with an initial cash balance of $45,059.70 and
initial inventory valuation of $4,940.30 (after seeding 40% catalog coverage).

Summary of Quantitative Outcomes:
  - Total Requests Processed: 20 / 20 (100% completion without runtime crashes)
  - Starting State (2025-04-01): Cash = $45,059.70 | Inventory = $4,940.30
  - Ending State (2025-04-17):   Cash = $44,330.89 | Inventory = $6,219.65
  - Cash Balance Changes: 12 distinct requests triggered cash balance updates
    (Requests #1, #2, #3, #5, #6, #7, #9, #10, #11, #12, #13, #15, and #19)
    through customer sales revenue and automated supplier restock purchases
    (exceeding the rubric minimum of 3).
  - Fully Fulfilled Orders: 4 complete multi-item orders were finalized and
    scheduled for on-time delivery (Requests #10, #11, #12, and #13), plus
    partial line-item sales recorded on Requests #1 and #15 (exceeding the
    rubric minimum of 3).
  - Properly Rejected / Unfulfilled Orders: 16 requests were declined in full
    or held pending customer confirmation with clear, specific justifications
    (exceeding the rubric minimum of 1).

Identified System Strengths:
  1. Accurate Constraint & Deadline Enforcement: The system reliably detects
     impossible delivery timelines using `get_supplier_delivery_date()`. For
     example, in Request #9 (deadline April 10, supplier ETA April 11) and
     Request #16 (deadline April 15, supplier ETA April 16-19), the agents
     correctly blocked fulfillment and explained the exact delivery date gap.
  2. Non-Catalog Item Rejection: Requests containing items not sold by Munder
     Difflin—such as "200 balloons" (Request #2), "500 reams of cardboard"
     (Request #15), and "10,000 tickets" (Request #20)—were immediately
     flagged as unfulfillable while still providing helpful bulk quotes for
     the valid catalog items in the inquiry.
  3. Transparent, Customer-Safe Responses: Every quote itemizes unit prices,
     subtotals, bulk discount tiers (5%, 10%, or 15%), and final totals.
     Furthermore, customer-facing responses contain zero leakage of sensitive
     internal data (no internal cash balances, supplier unit margins, or raw
     database errors).
  4. Proactive Inventory Replenishment: When stock levels dropped below order
     requirements and supplier lead times fit within customer deadlines (e.g.,
     Requests #2, #6, #7, #19), the Inventory Agent automatically placed
     `stock_orders` transactions, growing total inventory value from $4,940.30
     to $6,219.65 while preserving healthy liquidity ($44,330.89).


3. SUGGESTIONS FOR FURTHER SYSTEM IMPROVEMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Based on edge cases observed in `test_results.csv`, two concrete improvements
would further strengthen accuracy and order conversion rates:

  Improvement 1: Atomic All-or-Nothing Order Commit & Partial-Order Confirmation
  - Observation from Evaluation: In Requests #1 and #15, the Sales Agent
    recorded individual line-item `sales` transactions for available items
    (e.g., Cardstock and Colored Paper in Request #1) even though another item
    in the same request (A4 Glossy Paper) was unavailable and the Orchestrator
    told the customer the full order could not be finalized yet.
  - Proposed Enhancement: Implement a two-phase commit pattern (or a
    `validate_full_order` pre-check before calling `create_transaction`) so
    multi-item orders are committed atomically only when 100% of requested line
    items pass stock and deadline checks, unless the customer explicitly opts
    into partial fulfillment.

  Improvement 2: Unit-of-Measure Conversion & Substitution Rules Engine
  - Observation from Evaluation: Customers occasionally request quantities in
    different packaging units (e.g., "500 reams of printer paper" in Request #3,
    where 1 ream = 500 sheets) or use ambiguous product names (e.g., Request
    #3 substituted "A3 Paper" with "Colored paper", and Request #1 flagged
    "A4 Glossy Paper" as uncarried despite "Glossy paper" existing in the
    catalog).
  - Proposed Enhancement: Add a structured unit-normalization layer (converting
    reams/boxes/packs to base catalog units) and expand `ITEM_ALIASES` with
    fuzzy/embedding-based catalog matching plus explicit customer approval
    prompts before substituting similar paper grades.
